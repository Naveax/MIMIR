chosen first prototype skill family
- low boost recovery

why this is the first prototype
- It is frequent enough to source replay slices without turning data acquisition into the blocker.
- It is mechanically structured around car-state recovery, ground/air transition control, and resource pressure rather than rare highlight mechanics.
- It can be canonicalized with less dependence on full ball/opponent tactical semantics than flick-heavy families.
- It supports clear preconditions, failure modes, abstain boundaries, and recovery hooks.
- It has obvious downstream value: recovery under low resources is broadly useful and not demo-only.
- It allows the vertical slice to prove replay slice -> skill object -> curriculum -> export -> eval without needing full real-physics rollout first.

candidate ranking
1. low boost recovery
   Why here:
   broad occurrence, clean staged value, clearer validation and curriculum path, lower semantic coupling.
2. double wave dash recovery
   Why second:
   strong mechanic and still recovery-oriented, but narrower and more timing-sensitive; better as the next family after the first recovery pipeline is proven.
3. flick escape
   Why third:
   more ball-coupled, more context-sensitive, and less suitable for the first narrow replay-slice compiler pass.
4. rare flick family
   Why deferred:
   data sparsity, high tactical-context dependence, wider family ambiguity, and higher risk of fake generalization from isolated clips.

why rare flicks are deferred
- They are too sparse for a first vertical slice.
- Canonicalization boundaries are less stable because ball contact, opponent pressure, and tactical intent matter more.
- Anti-target definition is harder: a near-neighbor mechanic can look similar while demanding different timing and control.
- The risk of overfitting one replay seed into a misleading "skill" is substantially higher.

what success looks like for low boost recovery
- A replay slice can be compiled into one reusable low-boost-recovery skill object with explicit provenance.
- The canonicalizer and phase extractor can explain the seed without inventing missing semantics.
- The parameter solver can produce bounded recovery parameters that survive into validated variants.
- The validator can reject unreachable or unsafe variants and surface abstain reasons.
- A curriculum can be emitted that progresses from easy recovery states to harder low-resource states.
- An export shell exists for the first consumer path.
- Eval shows baseline vs intervention metrics for recovery success, time-to-stable-control, and failure-rate reduction on the chosen prototype task.

follow-on implication
- If the low boost recovery slice proves out cleanly, double wave dash recovery is the next justified family because it reuses much of the same recovery-first architecture while increasing mechanical sharpness.
