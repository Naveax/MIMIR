why low boost recovery is the only target family here
- It is the explicitly chosen first Skill Forge v1 prototype family.
- The staged-delivery rules require one family-specific replay-slice-to-export seed path first.
- A universal replay-slice contract now would force speculative fields for families with no first consumer.
- Low boost recovery keeps the seed boundary tied to one subject and one bounded recovery occurrence without reopening wider replay semantics.

exact required fields
- `slice_id`
- `source_replay_ref`
- `provenance_label`
- `frame_window.start`
- `frame_window.end_exclusive`
- `subject`
- `family_hint = low_boost_recovery`
- `raw_state_window_ref`

exact optional fields
- `audit_note`

exact rejected scope expansions
- real replay parsing
- replay corpus mining or search
- universal multi-family replay-slice semantics
- rollout or physics proof
- runtime bridge behavior
- export-bundle widening
- persistence/writer flow for replay slices
- broad metadata bags with no immediate consumer
- tactic-wide or outcome-wide inferred semantics

whether narrow Rust type shaping was added, and why
- Yes.
- Added minimal shared DTOs in `crates/mimir-types/src/lib.rs`:
  - `ReplaySliceId`
  - `ReplaySubjectRef`
  - `RawStateWindowRef`
  - `ReplaySliceFamilyHint`
  - `ReplaySourceRef`
  - `ReplaySliceRef`
- Reused existing `TimeWindow` and `FrameIndex` instead of creating parallel window/index types.
- This was justified because the repo had no existing code-local seed type for `ReplaySlice`, and the next canonicalizer/event-phase pass needs one auditable shared boundary without adding parser or persistence behavior.

why this is enough for the next canonicalizer/event-phase pass
- The next pass now has one explicit input boundary with identity, provenance, bounded frame scope, subject scope, family scope, and bounded raw-state linkage.
- That is sufficient to design family-specific canonicalization and extraction ownership without guessing hidden ingestion semantics.
- It intentionally does not prove low-boost semantics, contacts, or phases; those remain owned by the next pass.
